# Operation X — resource guide

Reviewed 18 November 2026. Links below are author, publisher, university, project or manufacturer sources. Chapter selections, difficulty and repository assignments are recommendations for this project. Free access to a companion site does not imply that the full commercial textbook is free. Link to third-party books; do not copy them into these repositories.

Repository abbreviations: **Journey** = semiconductor-learning-journey; **MOS** = mosfet-characterization; **ALU** = 8bit-alu-verilog; **ADC** = cmos-adc-design; **AI** = tiny-ai-accelerator. Every resource also supports cited notes in Journey. “Beginner” assumes the learner's current electrical-engineering background, rather than no mathematics or circuit knowledge.

## Keep the two primary books

| Resource and legal access | Read for Operation X | Prerequisites and difficulty | Practical output and repository |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harris & Harris, Digital Design and Computer Architecture, 2e.** Use your copy or institutional access. [Author companion site](https://pages.hmc.edu/harris/ddca/ddca2e.html) provides free slides, HDL, labs, errata and odd-exercise solutions; [publisher contents](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/digital-design-and-computer-architecture/harris/978-0-12-394424-5). | Ch. 1 number representation; Ch. 2 combinational logic; Ch. 4 HDL, especially testbenches; Ch. 5 arithmetic and registers. Add Ch. 3 for the MAC. Later Ch. 6–8 for ISA, microarchitecture and memory. | Boolean algebra and binary arithmetic; beginner → intermediate. Learn signed versus unsigned interpretation before modifying RTL. | Explain ALU flags, build a registered wrapper, write control FSMs and eventually an instruction-driven datapath. **ALU, AI**. This edition develops **MIPS**, so do not label its processor chapters as RISC-V. |
| **Weste & Harris, CMOS VLSI Design, 4e.** Use your copy or institutional access. [Author contents and selected free chapters](https://pages.hmc.edu/harris/cmosvlsi/4e/lookinside.html). | Ch. 2 MOS theory and Ch. 8 simulation first; Ch. 9–11 combinational, sequential and datapath design next. Then Ch. 4–7 delay, power, interconnect and robustness; Ch. 12 arrays; Ch. 14–15 methodology and verification. | MOS I–V, RC circuits, logic and HDL; intermediate → advanced. | Connect MOS sizing to an inverter's delay, analyze adder/multiplier structures, and interpret mapped timing/area reports. **MOS, ALU, AI**, with device/simulation foundations for **ADC**. |

Do not read both cover to cover before building. For the next month, use Harris Ch. 2/4/5 alongside Hu Ch. 5/6. Open Weste Ch. 2/8 when interpreting the first transistor simulation.

## Manufacturer references for the physical options

- [Microchip 2N7000 datasheet](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/APID/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/2N7000-N-Channel-Enhancement-Mode-Vertical-DMOS-FET-Data-Sheet-20005695A.pdf): use pinout, ratings, electrical-characteristic test conditions and typical curves. Prerequisites: power/current calculations; beginner lab level. Supports **MOS** measurements. The vertical discrete transistor is not the same device geometry as a planar IC MOSFET or the supplied Level-1 example.
- [TI LM339-family datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lm339.pdf): use the exact part's supply/common-mode limits, offset, open-collector output and application guidance. Prerequisites: comparators and logic levels; beginner/intermediate lab level. Supports **ADC** breadboard planning. Choose a through-hole variant or adapter deliberately; do not assume all family suffixes have the same package or specifications.

## Reading order and proof of learning

1. **Now:** Harris 2/4/5, Hu 5/6 and the existing RTL reports. Explain three ALU edge cases and one MAC wrap case without running the code first.
2. **First device project:** Hu 1/2/4 as needed, Weste 2/8, MIT 6.012 targeted notes. Produce actual I–V data and a model-limits paragraph.
3. **First converter:** Kester 2/3/5 and the datasheet for the chosen comparator. Predict thresholds before simulation.
4. **Next:** Harris 3/6–8 and Sze metrics/kernels/mapping for the array; Razavi 2–7 for the transistor comparator.
5. **Later specialization:** Tsividis/DEVSIM for device modeling, or Weste/LibreLane for VLSI. Deep quantum transport and emerging-device papers follow electrostatics and drift-diffusion competence; they are not prerequisites for your first verified projects.

For each reading, create one derivation, one solved exercise and one prediction tested by an experiment. A completed reading checkbox alone is not engineering evidence.
